{% comment %} The home page's playground: a pattern, a subject, the PHP version, and what the real PHP and PCRE2 say about them, run in the visitor's browser by a PHP compiled to WebAssembly with PHPRegex loaded into it. Before the visitor asks for a run, every tab shows the sample stored in _data/playground.json, rendered by the same script as a live result; the engine is downloaded on the first run gesture only, never on load. The data is embedded as JSON. Liquid writes "<" back when it re-encodes, so the include escapes it: a "<" could otherwise close the script element. A window: the title bar holds the PHP version, Run and the state; the examples stand in a column of their own, each carrying its pattern under its name, then what the visitor writes (the pattern, the text); on the right, what the engine answers, behind one strip of tabs. Under 1280px the column folds back into a row above the fields, one a phone scrolls; under 1024px the window runs on one column, the answers after the text. The tree is the first tab, the one the home's title promises, and the explanation its other voice; then the diagnostics, and what the console does with a pattern: the ReDoS verdict with its attack string, the translation to JavaScript, HTML or Python, sample subjects, the pattern laid out over lines, and the match stepped through as PCRE2 itself ran it (the real PCRE2, compiled to WebAssembly, in a worker of its own). The matches are said by the text field itself, underlined where they land, their count spoken by the live line; no tab holds them. Two tabs carry a badge, so the state of the answer reads on a closed tab: the errors and the lint findings, the verdict. A tool asks the engine when its tab is opened, the verdict on every run; never on every keystroke. The pattern field is colored by the same answer: the engine's token spans, drawn in a mirror under the field, the code blocks' palette; it falls back to the field's own ink whenever the mirror does not render what the field says. The tree's pattern, piece by piece, wears the same colors inside its pieces. Three gestures greet the first visit: a caret blinks at the end of the pattern until the field is touched, so it reads as editable; a light walks the tree top to bottom, one node at a time, until a hand reaches for the tree or a field; and the engine's first boot, a download of a few megabytes, is announced by a loader over the window's body, so what is coming is said rather than guessed. The status bar frames the window from the first paint: what stays on the visitor's machine, then the engine that answered. The notes and the credits sit under the window. {% endcomment %}
